# Step V1-1 — Trees

## What the code provides (2026-11-25)
- `Vegetation/TreeGrowth` (pure, deterministic): the skeleton of a tree.
  - Trunk: runs to the top (`Excurrent` ≥ 0.5: beech, hornbeam, birch, alder)
    or splits into 2–4 limbs low in the crown (oak).
  - Main branches along the trunk (`BranchesPerM`), at the genus angle
    (`BranchAngleDeg`, flatter low in the crown, steeper at the top), then
    secondary branches and twigs alternately left and right.
  - Every branch bends up (`UpCurve`), hangs (`Droop`, birch twigs), kinks
    (`Tortuosity`, oak) and stops at the crown envelope of the genus
    (`CrownRadiusM`, `CrownPeak`, `CrownExponent`, `Lumpiness`).
  - Leaves on the twigs and the outer part of their parents, budget per form
    (600 / 5,000 / 10,000 / 13,000; lobed leaves 60 %).
- `VegetationMeshBuilder`: tapered tubes for the wood (bark colors, ridges,
  fluting, moss as before), double-sided leaf blades shaped by `LeafWidth`,
  `LeafTip`, `LeafLobes`, sized `LeafSizeCm × LeafScale`. Vertex color alpha
  of a leaf = a random value per leaf (for the leaf fall, M2).
- About 100k triangles for a mature tree, 150k for an old one (Nanite).
- Test `ShroomHunt.Vegetation.TreeGrowth`: determinism, branches inside the
  crown, thinner children, leaf budget, trunk type per genus.
- The Megascans mapping of 2026-11-23/24 is removed from `DefaultGame.ini`.

# Step V1-2 — Forest floor (done 2026-11-25, D-042)

A real forest floor is a continuous carpet of dead leaves, also on rides and
footpaths, with plants growing through it: this comes from the **ground
material** (the landscape layers), not from 3D objects. The 3D leaves of V1-3
only add relief on top.

## What the code provides
- `Environment/GroundTextures` (pure, deterministic): draws one tileable
  stylized texture per landscape layer from `Data/Environment/GroundTextures.json`:
  base color and noise, then fallen leaves (shapes and colors of the genera of
  `Vegetation.json`), moss bumps, grass blades, pebbles, twigs, puddles.
  Albedo alpha = height (for the height blend), plus a normal map.
- The editor bakes them at startup when the data changed, like the vegetation
  (`T_Ground_<Layer>_D` / `_N` in `Environment/Landscape/Generated`, not
  versioned); `sh.Ground.BakeTextures` forces it, `sh.Ground.Preview` writes
  only PNGs to `Saved/GroundTextures/`.
- `Tools/Python/create_ground_material.py` (already run): the material function
  `MF_GroundLayers` blends the six layers (height blend, shared wrap samplers)
  and outputs **BaseColor**, **Normal**, **Roughness**.
- Test `ShroomHunt.Environment.GroundTextures`: determinism, seamless tiling,
  normals, a closed leaf carpet.

![Generated ground layers](../Media/ground_textures.png)

> **UE concept — Height blend.** A *Landscape Layer Blend* in *LB Height
> Blend* mode uses a height per layer: where two layers meet, the higher
> pixels win (leaves and pebbles stay on top of the soil) instead of a soft
> cross-fade.

## Editor steps (you)
1. Open the editor (the textures and meshes rebuild if needed, a few minutes).
2. Open `Content/ShroomHunt/Environment/Landscape/M_Landscape_Forest`.
3. Select the **Landscape Layer Blend** node and the color **Constant3Vector**
   nodes wired into it, press **Delete**. Note where its output went (usually
   the *Base Color* input of `MF_Wetness`) and where the roughness constant
   went.
4. Right-click the graph, type `MF_GroundLayers`, pick it (a *Material
   Function Call* node with three outputs appears).
5. Wire **BaseColor** where the Layer Blend output was, **Roughness** where the
   roughness constant was (into `MF_Wetness` too if it has a roughness input),
   and **Normal** to the **Normal** pin of the *Substrate Shading Models* node
   (the node that feeds *Front Material*). Delete the old roughness constant.
6. **Apply**, **Save** (shaders compile: wait).
7. In `Lvl_F01_LowlandOakBeech`, look at the ground in a stand, on a ride, on a
   road and by a stream. If the relief looks inverted (lit from below), open
   any `T_Ground_*_N` texture and tell me: I will flip the green channel in
   the generator.

# Step V1-2b — Ground relief (Nanite tessellation, 2026-11-26)

The ground textures carry their height (albedo alpha): leaves, moss cushions,
pebbles, puddles. **Nanite tessellation** turns it into real geometry: the
landscape is subdivided on screen and each point is pushed up by the height.
Snow becomes a real layer whose depth follows the snow pack.

## What the code provides
- `MF_GroundLayers` has a fourth output, **Height** (0–1), the sum of layer
  weight × layer height. It uses *Landscape Layer Sample* nodes, not a *Layer
  Blend*: a Layer Blend flattened the displacement in 5.8.0/5.8.1 (forum
  reports; you have 5.8.4).
- `MPC_Season.SnowDepthCm`: snow depth in cm (1 mm of snow water ≈ 1 cm of
  fresh snow), eased like the cover by the Environment Director.
- `r.Nanite.Tessellation` is already on by default in 5.8.

> **UE concept — Nanite tessellation and displacement.** A Nanite mesh (here the
> landscape) can be finely subdivided at render time; the material's
> *Displacement* input (0–1) moves each new point along the normal by
> (value − *Center*) × *Magnitude*. Geometry detail without heavy meshes.

## Editor steps (you)
1. Open `M_Landscape_Forest`. Select the result node (or click an empty spot)
   > **Details** > section **Nanite**:
   - tick **Enable Tessellation**;
   - **Displacement** > **Magnitude** `0.35`, **Center** `0.4`. On a landscape
     the displacement is in the landscape's local units and then scaled by the
     actor (scale 100: one unit = 1 m), so `0.35` = 35 cm per unit of height
     (`10` gave 10 m spikes). It must match `ReliefRangeCm` in
     `Data/Environment/GroundTextures.json` (35): the textures store real
     centimeters (×`ReliefScale` per layer: leaves ×3, pebbles ×1);
   - tick **Displacement Fade** (keep its default distances: far away the
     relief would not be seen anyway).
2. **Displacement input**: take the **Height** output of `MF_GroundLayers` >
   **Add** > **Displacement** pin of the result node. The Add's **B** = snow:
   your snow mask (step 3 of M2, after the patchy noise of 3.6) **Multiply**
   **Collection Parameter** `MPC_Season.SnowDepthCm` **Multiply** `0.0286`
   (= 1/35: cm → units of 35 cm, the Magnitude).
3. **Apply**, **Save** (shaders compile).
4. In `Lvl_F01_LowlandOakBeech`, select the **Landscape** > **Details** >
   tick **Enable Nanite**, then section **Nanite** > **Build Data** (or the
   **Build** menu > **Build Landscape**). Wait, **Save All**.
5. Look at the ground at a low angle, in a stand and on a road; then
   `sh.Weather.Force Snow 6` and `sh.Time.Scale 60` in Play: the snow layer
   should thicken and hide the leaves.

If the ground looks flat, sinks or floats, or has black flickering patches,
take a screenshot and tell me (5.8 had displacement bugs; we have fallbacks).

# Step V1-3 — Ground debris

Done before V1-1/V1-2 (the tree packs were still downloading): it needs no
third-party asset.

## What the code provides
- A third vegetation layer, **Ground**, with four kinds in the `Ground` list of
  `Data/Vegetation/Vegetation.json` (same fields as the understory rules, plus
  `SlopeBoost`, `BankBoost` and `PathFactor`):
  - **Leaf**: single fallen leaves placed **one by one** (no thinning, ~7 per m²
    under closed stands), one mesh set per genus (`Litter` color, `LeafSizeCm`,
    `LeafLobes` of each tree archetype: copper beech, lobed oak, yellow birch,
    brown hornbeam, dark alder; 4 variants each). The genus is drawn from the
    stand weights at the spot, so the leaves match the trees above. Also on
    rides (x0.6) and footpaths (x0.4), never on gravel roads
    (`PathFactor` [road, ride, footpath]). They add relief on top of the
    continuous leaf carpet of the ground texture (V1-2);
  - **Twigs**: dead twigs, more under old stands;
  - **Stones**: half-sunk mossy stones, more on slopes and along streams;
  - **Moss**: cushions on moist ground.
- Ground debris lies flat on the terrain slope, never in the water, skips the
  trunk test (tiny, hidden by trunks anyway) and has no collision.
- About **8 million leaves** for F01 (`sh.Vegetation.Stats` estimates them from
  1 ha): far too many to store, so this layer is **generated at runtime around
  the player** by PCG (hierarchical runtime generation, 32 m cells, ~40 m
  radius), each leaf at a deterministic position.
- Baked like the trees (`sh.Vegetation.BakeMeshes`): `SM_<Genus>_Leaf_<n>`,
  `SM_Twigs_<n>`, `SM_Stones_<n>`, `SM_Moss_<n>`; replaceable by Megascans debris
  through *Mesh Overrides* later (e.g. key `Fagus_Leaf`).
- Preview: `sh.Vegetation.ExportObj`, then
  `render_mushroom_preview.py Saved/VegetationDebug <out.png> --grid --top`.

![Ground debris placeholders](../Media/placeholder_ground_debris.png)

> **UE concept — PCG runtime generation.** A PCG component set to *Generate At
> Runtime* generates its graph only in a grid of cells around the player (the
> *generation source*) and cleans up the cells left behind, while playing.
> With *hierarchical generation* the graph runs per cell of a fixed grid size.
> Ideal for dense small detail: nothing is stored in the level.
